Nido Accesorios: campaigns that bring in candidates, visits and purchases

An Argentine mobile accessories chain, with 10 stores and a franchise program. I run its Meta and Google campaigns and its email marketing, measure everything that goes out, and deliver statistics and a guide for its social media.

Role
Freelance campaign and measurement consultant.
Year

Stack

Nido Accesorios is a fictional name: the client's name, logo and domain were replaced.

What the business needed

Ten stores, five owned and five franchised, an online store and a program to open in new cities. It asked marketing for three things:

  • Sell more in stores and online.
  • Find franchisees and staff to open new cities.
  • Know which campaign brought what, to decide the investment with numbers that can be read.

The franchise website, where every campaign lands

To find franchisees I built a franchise website: a hub, support pages (how much it costs, profitability, how to open, before signing, why a Nido franchise, cities and FAQ) and 15 city pages, each with its contact form and its WhatsApp button.

Measurement

  • Funnel in GA4 with custom events, and Microsoft Clarity on the franchise section.
  • WhatsApp clicks, Search Console and the conversion imported into Google Ads.
  • Internal traffic filter, so the numbers come from customers and not from the team.
  • All together in Campaign Suite, the dashboard I designed so the team can see the results.

Email

Email campaigns for the store calendar: Valentine's Day, back to school, the points program and the franchise. Designed in Beefree, phone first, with the product and the offer on the first screen.

How I work with the client

  • I start from the business need, not from a format: an opening, a job opening, a slow month, a new city. Each becomes a campaign with a goal.
  • Nothing goes live without approval. Campaigns are created paused, with the piece, the audience and the budget in a one-page plan.
  • Measurement is set up before the ad, so every result can be traced back to its campaign.
  • One report per month, in plain words: what was paid, what it brought in and what to do now.
