SEGA: a creator campaign to get Sonic running again

For the launch of “Sonic Generations Recharged”, a remastered version of the classic Sonic games, we built a creator campaign: ten profiles in three tiers, an outreach and value-exchange plan for each one, the brief they receive, the content guidelines, the timeline, the metrics and a reading of trends, hashtags and competitors.

The brief

SEGA is launching “Sonic Generations Recharged”. The campaign’s goal: light up the nostalgia of those who grew up with Sonic and, at the same time, capture those discovering him for the first time. The audience is 15 to 35, lives between YouTube, Twitch, Instagram and TikTok, and mixes video games with pop culture, anime and memes. The key message: Sonic is timeless; whether it is your first run or a throwback, the game is about speed, fun and reconnecting with what made gaming magical.

Ten creators in three tiers

The selection mixes sizes on purpose: mega creators (over a million followers) for reach and launch buzz, mid-tier (between a hundred thousand and a million) for niche credibility and in-depth content, and micro (under a hundred thousand) for authenticity and community. The categories cover gaming, retro, animation, cosplay and general entertainment, and Sonic’s official account acts as the central voice that orders the conversation.

A plan per creator

Each creator has three lines: how they are approached, what they get in return and what long-term relationship is sought. Jacksepticeye is reached through his agency and SEGA’s press team, with early access and a paid stream; in return, exclusive content, merchandise and affiliate-link commission; long term, a regular collaborator and even a place in a game’s credits. Pokimane is offered a live “Nostalgia Night” with interactive features and a Sonic avatar for her overlay; long term, a “Play With Poki” series with SEGA launches. ItsSunpi is invited by direct message to cosplay retro and modern Sonic, with a cosplay kit and a spot on SEGA’s global Instagram; long term, the brand’s cosplay ambassador.

What the creators receive: brief, guidelines and timeline

The brief speaks their language: a playful, bold, energetic, authentic and “a little nerdy” tone; the content has to feel like one of their regular posts with Sonic speeding through. Recommended format: reels or TikTok of 30 to 60 seconds, with the option of a YouTube or Twitch segment and up to three stories. Do: the game’s full title, the tag of Sonic’s account and the hashtag #RechargedWithSonic, bright lighting and references for fans. Don’t: competitors, touching the logo or the colors, fake testimonials, over-editing. The timeline has five stops, from the brief to a coordinated publication day, and payment is set by tier with extras: exclusive merchandise, affiliate links, early access, performance bonuses and long-term opportunities.

How it is measured

Three priority metrics: engagement rate (the most important: likes, shares, saves and comments that show excitement, nostalgia or curiosity), tag and hashtag usage (to track and repost the content from the official channels) and reach relative to each creator’s usual performance. Internally, story interactions, duets and stitches, traffic to SEGA pages through unique links, and comment tone are also tracked.

Trends, hashtags, competitors and mood

Four trends in the niche: nostalgia marketing (reissues and remasters), short video as the dominant format, creator-led launches that outperform traditional ads, and cosplay and fan art as drivers of organic buzz. The competitors in the mascot and reissue space are Nintendo, Capcom and PlayStation; Nintendo dominates the global conversation, so SEGA has to win with niche energy: Sonic’s meme value is its advantage. The mood is positive (“this brings back my childhood”), with a minor risk: the fear of bugs or underwhelming gameplay, as in previous remasters.

What I took away

That a creator campaign is not built with a list of names but with three tiers that do different things, and that the brief is the product: if it does not speak the creator’s language, there is no content that feels real.
