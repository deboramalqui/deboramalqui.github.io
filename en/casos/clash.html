Clash: the onboarding of an app that gathers all your benefits in one place

Clash is an Argentine app that gathers in one place the benefits a person already has: from the bank, the health plan, the fintech, the employer. I designed its onboarding end to end: research, definition, prototype and user tests.

Role
UX/UI designer, freelance. Research, definition, prototype and testing of the onboarding.
Year
2021
Stack
FigmaMazeMeetSurveysInterviewsA/B testing
Client
Rosental Inversiones, Rosario

What Clash is, and for whom

A benefit is a discount, a promotion or a chance to save. Almost everyone has several and does not use them: from the bank, the health plan, the digital wallet, work. Clash gathers them in a single app, on iOS and Android.

The app was born in Rosario inside Rosental Inversiones, a very well-known Rosario group with businesses in capital markets, real estate and linked companies. La Capital, the Rosario newspaper, covered the launch: Rosental created an app that concentrates all the promotions.

My brief was the onboarding: the path from the moment someone installs the app until they see their benefits. How do we ask a person for their banks, their health plan and their interests without losing them before they see the value?

The starting point

The onboarding the app had when I started, with its real screenshots: three promise screens, a six-field sign-up, a code by email and a home that only then asks for the benefits.

I audited it screen by screen: I marked every decision the system forces, every action, where people can drop off and where they have to leave the app to continue. Three drop-off points before seeing a single benefit: the long sign-up, the e-mail code and a home with nothing of their own.

The onboarding I designed

Two steps and a promise. First the services (banks, health plans, universities, phone and internet), with a search box and a “don’t see your bank? Request it”; then the categories of interest, as chips. A wait that says what the app is doing, and a home built from what the person chose.

I prototyped it in Figma, low fidelity and under a provisional name, and tested it with users before drawing anything in high fidelity. Here it is rebuilt with Clash’s identity and programmed: whatever looks like a control works.

The prototype goes through the whole path: first what the app had in 2021 (the three screens, the sign-up and the code) and, after the code, the onboarding I designed, up to the home.

  • Search and “Request it”.Nobody is left out because their bank is not on the list: they request it and the app lets them know when it arrives.
  • Check mark and counter.Every selection is visible and counted: in the tests people asked for clear feedback and a way to verify what they added.
  • Search and “Explore”.Two ways to reach a benefit, searching or browsing, because both came up in the tests.

The research

Four weeks: review of the benefits, audit of the existing onboarding, stakeholder map, benchmarking, surveys and interviews.

What a benefit really is

I started by taking the object apart. Its many attributes are what make the system complex: until you understand what a benefit is, you cannot decide what to ask the person and in what order.

A benefit, taken apart

  • Stackable?
  • Cap?
  • Minimum purchase?
  • Free shipping
  • Monday to Thursday
  • While stocks last
  • 2 for 1
  • Points redemption
  • Applies throughcard, program, wallet, platform, health plan or coupon
  • Accessemployees, customers, members
  • Availabilitya period, certain days of the week, while stocks last
  • Condition2 for 1, free shipping, financing, points redemption, discount with a minimum purchase

Six dimensions and dozens of combinations: that is why the system is complex and why the onboarding has to ask for little, and ask well.

Benchmarking insight

  • None focuses on the benefit.They focus on the product and, by extension, on the category: when, where or what to buy.
  • None connects with the aggregators.The bank, the health plan and the employer are left out: that is Clash’s place.
  • All of them empower with information.Tools to understand what was spent, plan what comes next and decide. The onboarding has to teach that value while it collects data.

Interviews

The interviews surfaced the relationship between income and benefits, and it is not linear.

Relationship between monthly income and benefits

  • It is not linear.It depends on interest, time invested, habit, education and the information the person has about the possible benefits.
  • When income risesthe possible benefits increase, they are even “better”, and the person can choose. Frequency of use depends on interest and habit.
  • When income fallsaccess falls and frequency rises: supermarket cards, coupons and the most popular benefits.
  • The goalis to manage to buy a product, or to buy a product in order to buy another: miles, points.

A benefit is worth it for two reasons and at two moments: when it coincides with a need and, above all, when it coincides with a sacrifice, like a big purchase or one in installments.

The definition

With all of that I built the empathy map, the mind map, the clusters and the insight table, and from there came two personas and the flow.

PERSONA 1

“I need to save on everything I can”.

Mother of three, everyone works. Buying things is hard for them, but they manage. She learned to always look for the best deal.

Pain
“I’m poor”. “There are people above who cheat you or take advantage”. “I got used to watching my spending all day long”.

PERSONA 2

“I don’t have time”.

He is 28 and supports himself. He studies, works, runs around. He knows there are discounts, but doesn’t pay attention to them.

Pain
“I don’t realize I could pay less”. “Everything about the bank bores me”. “I almost always find out too late”.

The onboarding flow: welcome, authentication, choose the aggregators, choose the interests, choose the notification channels, an animation while the app searches for benefits, and the home. “How it works” stays within reach from the welcome screen for those who want to understand before signing up.

User testing and A/B

Two hypotheses: that people manage to connect their services and choose their preferences with a positive experience, and that they understand Clash’s value during the onboarding.

How I tested it

A Figma prototype, sessions over Meet with a silent observer, and role play: each participant received a profile with their banks, their health insurance and their phone company, and the task of creating the account. In Maze I integrated the five-second test and the Borg perceived-effort scale; to that I added think-aloud and the echo, boomerang and Columbo facilitation techniques.

MeasureHow it is scored
Success rate1 if they complete the task, 0.5 if they complete it only partly, 0 if they do not finish it.
EfficiencyTime spent on the task, average in seconds.
Quick understandingResult of the five-second test: 1 if they understood, 0.5 if they hesitated, 0 if they did not understand.
Perceived effortA direct question on a scale, from very light to very heavy, and why.
Quotes and notesClustered at the end, with triangulation of observers and of methods.

What I found

  • GoalConfirm a positive user experience and identify breaks in the expected flow.
  • Task for the user“Set it up so I can see my benefits”.
  • ParticipantsFour, chosen with a two-way table: money and time. Whether they read about benefits or not; whether they support children, support themselves or someone supports them.

Insights

  • Need to verify their data
  • Request for clearer feedback
  • They expect a home tailored to them
  • Search or explore: both

What I took away

  • The object before the screen. Until I took apart what a benefit is (cap, condition, access, category) I could not decide what to ask the person for, and in what order.
  • Measuring the qualitative. Four people are enough to find where a flow breaks, if every task has a success rate, a time and a perceived effort, and if two observers compare notes.
  • Test before painting. The low-fidelity prototype, under a provisional name, let me change the flow without attachment. What you see above is what remained after the tests.
